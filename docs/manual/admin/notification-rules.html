Notification rules and email

Deciding who is told what, and pointing the product at a mail server.

The product announces 23 things that can happen to a request. Each announcement has a rule behind it saying who is told and what the message says, and this screen is where those rules live.

The sub-line states the split the whole screen is built on: in-app notifications always fire; email is opt-in per event. Nobody can be cut out of the in-app notification centre, and nobody gets an email until you switch that event on.

Two different screens

This is not the same screen as the bell in your own sidebar. Notification rules is the editor; Notifications is your own centre, showing what the product has told you.

Email delivery

Nothing is emailed anywhere until the product knows about a mail server. The Email delivery block at the top is where you tell it.

FieldWhat it is for
SMTP hostOptionalThe mail server. The placeholder shows the shape — smtp.example.com.
PortOptionalThe port the server listens on.
UsernameOptionalThe account the product authenticates as.
From nameOptionalThe display name on the messages the product sends.
From addressOptionalThe address the messages come from.
Base URL for linksOptionalWhere the product is reachable, so the links inside an email go somewhere. The placeholder is https://budget.example.com.
Management copy listOptionalComma-separated addresses. The hint says exactly when they are used: "Copied on approvals and on urgent requests once they are approved."

Where the password lives

There is no password field on this screen, and its absence is deliberate. The SMTP password is read from the FERVID_SMTP_PASSWORD environment variable on the server and is never stored in the database. If mail is not going out and everything here looks right, that variable is the first thing to check with whoever runs the server.

  1. Fill in the mail server fields.
  2. Select Save email settings.
  3. Type an address in Send a test email to and select Send a test email.

    Your own address is filled in already. A successful send reports "Test email sent to …"; a failure reports the transport error rather than pretending it worked.

Send the test before you enable email on any event. A test that fails costs you nothing; twenty-three events quietly failing to deliver costs you a week of people saying they were never told.

Editing one rule

  1. Select Edit on the event’s row.
  2. Tick Also send an email for this event if you want mail as well as the in-app notification.

    Leaving it clear does not silence the event. The in-app notification still fires.

  3. Choose the audience under Who it goes to.

    Three tick boxes: The requester, The approver and The Accounts group. They are resolved per request, so "the approver" means whoever is actually approving that one.

  4. Add fixed addresses in Always also send To and Always copy (Cc) if the event has a standing audience.

    Comma-separated. These are added to the audience above rather than replacing it.

  5. Edit the Subject and the Message.
  6. Select Save rule.

    The panel closes and the row updates. Cancel abandons the change.

Writing the subject and message

The wording is yours. Placeholders in double braces are replaced with the request’s own values when the message goes out. The fourteen available are listed under the message box on the screen itself:

PlaceholderWhat it becomes
{{number}}The request number.
{{amount}}The amount asked for.
{{approved_amount}}The amount actually approved.
{{payee}}Who is being paid.
{{requester}}Who raised it.
{{approver}}Who is deciding it.
{{project}}The project.
{{head}}The expense head.
{{purpose}}What the request is for.
{{status}}Where the request has got to.
{{needed_by}}The required-by date.
{{submitted_on}}When it was submitted.
{{processing_on}}When it was taken for processing.
{{link}}A link straight to the request, built from the Base URL above.

A typo fails on save

Anything else in double braces is rejected when you save, and the screen tells you so. A typo cannot reach an inbox as an empty gap that nobody can debug afterwards — it fails here, while you are looking at it.

Always put {{link}} in the message. An email telling somebody a request needs them, with no way to open it, is an email that generates a reply asking where it is.

Can I turn off in-app notifications for an event?

No. The In-app column is a fixed On pill rather than a switch. Email is the only per-event delivery choice.

Nothing is arriving by email.

Check three things in order: the mail server settings save, the Send a test email result, and whether the event itself has Email switched on.

The test email failed with a message I do not understand.

It is the error the mail server gave back, passed on rather than swallowed. Hand it to whoever runs your mail — it is the fastest thing they can act on.

A workflow action happened but nobody was told.

Delivery failures never fail the action itself — an approval succeeds whether or not the email goes. The in-app notification is written before any email is attempted, so check the recipient’s notification centre before you conclude nothing fired.

Who is in "the Accounts group"?

Everybody whose roles carry the payment Process permission. It is not a list you maintain here — you change it by changing roles on Roles and permissions or by changing who holds them on People and accounts.
