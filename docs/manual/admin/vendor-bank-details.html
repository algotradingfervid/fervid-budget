Vendor bank details

The most sensitive data in the product, and who is allowed to see it.

An account number and an IFSC are the two pieces of data that turn a request into money leaving a bank. They are held on the vendor record, and they are the one block in the product with their own permission — separate from the permission that opens the rest of the vendor.

The consequence is that "can see vendors" and "can see how to pay them" are two different grants, and most people are meant to hold only the first.

Where the block is

On a vendor you are allowed to see it, the block is headed Payment details — restricted and opens with a padlocked banner: "Visible only with vendor bank permission. They are deliberately kept off the employee request form — an employee raising a request never sees or types them."

The fields

FieldWhat it is for
Account nameOptionalThe name the account is held in. It will not always match the vendor name.
Account numberOptionalRecorded as given.
IFSCOptionalRecorded as given.
BankOptionalThe bank.
BranchOptionalThe branch.
UPI IDOptionalOptional, and marked as such on the form.
Default modeOptionalNot set, Bank transfer — NEFT, RTGS or Cheque. The mode you would normally pay this vendor by.
Payment terms (days)OptionalHow many days the vendor is normally given. A number.
  1. Open the vendor from Vendors.
  2. Scroll to Payment details — restricted.
  3. Fill in or correct the fields.
  4. Select Save vendor.

    The bank block is saved with the rest of the record, in the same submission.

Who sees what

There are two grants, not one, and they combine into three states. They are set on the Vendor bank details row of Roles and permissions, which offers a View column and an Edit column and nothing else.

The role holdsWhat the person sees on a vendor
Neither grantA block headed Payment details carrying a padlocked banner: "You do not have permission to see bank details — Accounts maintains them. The record is hidden, not just the buttons."
View onlyThe real block, filled in, with every field greyed out and unchangeable.
View and editThe real block, editable, saved along with the rest of the record.

Why the block does not disappear instead

The refusal banner is deliberately shown instead of the block vanishing. A missing section leaves a reader wondering whether the vendor has no bank details or whether they are not allowed to see them; the banner answers that question.

Hidden, not merely not shown

For somebody without the view grant, the bank columns are never read out of the database at all. It is not a hidden field on a page that quietly holds the number — there is nothing on the page to find. That is the guarantee worth understanding before you decide who gets the grant.

View without edit

A field that is greyed out is not submitted by the browser, and the save ignores it in any case. Somebody with view but not edit cannot change a bank detail by any route the screen offers.

Where the details deliberately are not

The vendor picker on the request form returns no bank details to anybody, whatever their role. A requester choosing a vendor gets the name, the GSTIN and the city, and nothing else — the picker cannot become a side door onto the restricted block.

This is why the banner says the details are kept off the employee request form. An employee raising a request never sees or types an account number, so a mistyped one cannot reach a payment from that direction.

Who should hold it

That is your organisation’s decision rather than the product’s, so this page will not pretend to make it. What the product gives you is the ability to make it precisely: the grant is separable from vendor viewing, from vendor editing and from paying, so "the accounts team maintains bank details and nobody else sees them" is a policy you can actually implement.

Backups carry them too

A backup contains the bank block in full, because it is a copy of the database. Anybody who can reach the backup folder on the server has the account numbers, whatever their role inside the product says. See Backups.

I can open a vendor but the Payment details block is padlocked.

Your role does not hold the vendor bank view grant. It is a different grant from the one that let you open the vendor.

I can see the bank details but cannot type in them.

You hold view and not edit. Both are on the Vendor bank details row of Roles and permissions.

Does the accounts team need this grant to record a payment?

Recording a payment does not require it — the payment form asks for the mode and the reference, not the account number. Whether your accounts team needs to read the details to make the transfer is a question about how they bank, not about the product.

Are bank details written into the audit trail?

No. A vendor save writes an audit row carrying the name, type, status, GSTIN and city, and for the bank block only a bank_changed flag saying whether it was touched. The trail records that somebody changed the details, not what they changed them to.
