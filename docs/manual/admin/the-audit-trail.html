Manual / Administration

The audit trail

Who did what, when — the record that cannot be edited.

Every act that changes a financial record leaves a row here, with the person who did it, the time they did it, and — where there is one — the before and the after. The screen’s own sub-line puts it plainly: review who changed financial records, when, and why.

It is a record and not a workspace. There is nothing on this screen that changes anything, because the value of the trail is that it cannot be tidied up afterwards.

The screen

ColumnWhat it holds
WhenThe date and time of the act, to the minute.
ActorWho did it, by name.
EntityWhat kind of thing was touched — User, Month Lock, Payment, Vendor and so on.
ActionWhat was done to it, as a coloured pill.
SummaryA sentence describing the act, and a Before / after expander where there is stored detail. A role row lists the permission changes instead (see below).

The capture above shows two month-lock entries in among the sign-ins: "Locked 2026-03: Month closed for reporting." and "Unlocked 2026-03: Reopened after capturing the manual." The text after each colon is the reason somebody typed at the time. That is the whole argument for requiring a reason on a month lock — a year later it is the only thing that explains the row.

A change to a role's permissions is shown differently. Instead of a Before / after expander, the Summary lists the individual permissions and data scopes that were added or removed, by name rather than as a count, and — if you can view roles — an Open affected role link. Use the actor and time to identify who granted or removed access.

Before and after

Where the product stored the record’s state, the Summary carries a Before / after disclosure. Open it and you get the stored values as they were and as they became. Not every action has one — a sign-in has nothing to compare — so the expander appears only on rows that can fill it.

Finding something

  1. Choose an Entity if you know what kind of record you are after.
  2. Choose an Action if you know what was done to it.
  3. Type a name into Actor to narrow to one person. It matches on any part of the name.
  4. Pick a From date, a To date, or both, to narrow to a period. Both days are included in full.
  5. Select Filter.
  6. Select Reset to clear everything and start again.

The entities you can filter by

The list reads exactly as the screen writes it: Payment Request, Payment, Budget, Budget Month, Month Lock, Project, Head, Vendor, Recoverable Category, Recoverable Report, User, Role, Notification Setting, App Setting, Report, Backup.

The actions you can filter by

The list is ordered so the request workflow reads as a workflow rather than as an alphabet, and each action appears under the same label as its pill in the Action column: Created, Updated, Deleted, Attached, Commented, then Submitted, Approved, Returned, Rejected, Withdrawn, Raised again, then Cancellation asked, Cancelled, Approval reassigned, then Reserved, Released, Reservation reassigned, Put on hold, Taken off hold, then Settled, Marked partial, Partial accepted, Concern raised, Reminder sent, Recovery recorded, then Voided, Locked, Unlocked, Settings saved, Exported, and finally Logged in, Logged out, Login failed.

Two filters are usually enough. "Everything Fervid Admin did to a Month Lock" is Entity plus Actor, and it answers most of the questions people actually bring to this screen. Add a From and To date when you know roughly when it happened.

What the trail cannot do

The record cannot be edited

Nothing on this screen edits or removes a row, and there is no route anywhere in the product that does. An entry that is wrong stays; the correction is a new act, which leaves its own entry beside it. That is what makes the trail worth reading.

One hundred rows to a page

The screen shows 100 entries at a time, newest first. When there are more, a line under the table says which ones you are looking at — for example Showing 1–100 of 2345 — with Older → to go further back and ← Newer to come forward again. The paging keeps every filter you set, so moving between pages never widens the search. The filters work over the whole trail, not only the page you are on.

Rows you are not allowed to see

If your role cannot see every request and every payment, rows about the requests and payments outside your reach are left out of the list altogether, and the count under the table counts only what you can see. Rows about budgets, users, settings and the rest are shown to everybody who can open the screen.

Who should be able to read it

The trail carries the summaries and stored values of financial records, so treat it as sensitive. Vendor bank details are the one thing deliberately kept out of it: a vendor save records the vendor's profile — name, type, status, GSTIN, PAN, contact details, address and so on — and for the bank block only a flag saying whether it was touched. See Vendor bank details.

What lands in it

Not everything the product does, but everything that changes a record somebody may later have to account for. Among them: creating and editing users and roles, saving configuration, saving a budget, opening a month, locking and unlocking a month, creating and updating projects, heads and vendors, saving and deleting recoverable categories, recording, editing and voiding payments, every decision on a request, taking a backup, and signing in and out.

Some acts are recorded in more than one place, and deliberately so. A month lock writes a row here and shows its reason on the variance grid; a payment void writes a row here and keeps its reason on the payment. The trail is the copy that survives when a record is changed again.

Can I export the audit trail?

There is no export control on this screen. The filters are how you narrow it, and the browser is how you read it.

Somebody says they did not do something, but their name is on a row.

The name is the account that was signed in. If it is genuinely not them, that is a password question — reset it from People and accounts and look at the Logged in and Login failed rows around the time.

A change I made is not here.

Check the filters first — including From and To — then select Older → in case it is on a later page. If it is genuinely absent, the act may not be one the product records — not every read or navigation leaves a row, only changes do.

Why does one row have Before / after and the next one does not?

Because only some acts have a stored state worth comparing. A sign-in has nothing to compare; a budget save has an old figure and a new one. Role rows list their permission changes instead.

Can I stop somebody seeing this screen?

Yes. It is gated on the Audit · view permission, which sits behind the View cell of the Administration row in Roles and permissions. Open that row's Advanced list to take it away on its own.
