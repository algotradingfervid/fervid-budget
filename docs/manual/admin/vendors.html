Vendors

The vendor master, and the payment history behind each one.

The vendor master is the list of everybody the company pays that is not an employee. A requester picks from it rather than typing a name, so the same supplier is spelt the same way on every request, and the reporting can add them up.

A vendor record has four ordinary parts — identity, statutory numbers, contact details and notes — and one restricted part, the bank block, which is covered on its own page: Vendor bank details.

The vendor list

The line under the heading is a summary of the master itself: how many vendors are active, how many are missing a GSTIN, and the reminder that bank details are visible only with permission.

ColumnWhat it shows
VendorThe name, linking to the record. Its categories, if any, sit underneath.
TypeCompany, Proprietor or Individual.
GSTINThe number, or a Missing pill.
CityFrom the contact block, or an em dash.
Paid this yearWhat has actually gone out to this vendor.
Open requestsHow many requests naming this vendor are still in flight.
StatusActive or Inactive.

The row across the foot totals the two figures for whatever the filters left on screen, and says how many of the whole master you are looking at — "12 shown of 12" in the capture above.

The GSTIN banner

When any vendor has no GSTIN, a banner appears above the filters counting them. It is a nudge, not a block — the wording is explicit that "They can still be paid. The gap shows up in vendor reporting until someone fills it in." Show them filters the list down to exactly those vendors, and Show all puts it back.

Finding a vendor

FieldWhat it is for
SearchOptionalMatches on name, GSTIN, PAN or city.
TypeOptionalAll types, Company, Proprietor or Individual.
CategoryOptionalThe categories that exist in the master, offered as a list.
StatusOptionalActive by default, or Inactive, or All. An inactive vendor will not appear until you change this.
  1. Fill in whichever filters narrow things down.
  2. Select Apply.

Adding a vendor

  1. Select ＋ Add vendor.
  2. Type the Vendor name. It must be unique across the master.
  3. Choose a Type.
  4. Fill in whatever else you have to hand.
  5. Select Save vendor.

    You land on the vendor’s own record. The note beside the buttons on the blank form says it plainly: the name must be unique, and everything else can be filled in later.

TDS is recorded, not calculated

The statutory block is a record, not a calculator. The hint beside it says so: the numbers are recorded for reference only, this system never computes tax, and Accounts confirms deductions outside it.

One vendor in detail

The record is the form. There is no read-only view and no separate edit screen — if your role carries the vendor edit permission you can change what you are looking at and select Save vendor; if it does not, every box is disabled and the only control is Back to vendors. The foot of the form carries the record’s dates: when it was created, and when it was last edited.

The Requests, Payments and History tabs

Three of the four tabs above the form — Requests, Payments and History — are marked Soon. They are announcements of screens that do not exist yet, not links, and selecting them does nothing. Only Record is built.

Until they are, the vendor-level figures you have are Paid this year and Open requests on the list, and the payments ledger filtered by hand.

Retiring a vendor

  1. Open the vendor.
  2. Change Status to Inactive.
  3. Select Save vendor.

The hint under the field is the whole rule: inactive vendors disappear from new requests but stay on old ones. Nothing already raised, approved or paid is touched, and the vendor is still reachable from the list once you set the Status filter to Inactive or All.

A vendor cannot be deleted. If a record was created twice by mistake, retire the wrong one and note in Internal notes which record replaced it — the requests already pointing at it keep pointing at it.

A requester says a vendor is missing from their picker.

The picker offers active vendors only. Check the record’s Status, and check the spelling — the picker matches on name, GSTIN and city.

Can a requester add a vendor themselves?

Only if their role carries the vendor create permission. The picker offers ＋ Add a new vendor to somebody who could actually complete it, and to nobody else.

Two vendors have the same name.

They cannot. The name is unique across the master and the check ignores case, so a duplicate save is refused with A record with this name already exists.

Why can I not see the bank block on a vendor?

It is a separate permission from the rest of the record. See Vendor bank details.
