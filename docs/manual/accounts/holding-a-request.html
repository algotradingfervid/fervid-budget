Manual / Paying

Putting a request on hold

Parking an approved request with a reason, and taking it off hold again.

A hold is what you use when a request is approved but you cannot pay it as it stands — the bank details do not match the vendor master, the invoice attached is the wrong one, a figure needs confirming. It is not a rejection and not a decision. It parks the request and asks the requester a question.

Only Accounts can place a hold, and only Accounts can lift one. While a request is held, nobody in Accounts — including you — can claim or pay it.

Placing a hold

  1. Open the request. Select Put on hold at the foot of the screen.

    The control appears only on an approved request that is not already held. A request somebody has claimed has to be released first.

  2. Write what you need from the requester.

    It is required, and the placeholder is the point: they see this exactly as you write it.

  3. Select Put on hold.

    The requester is asked to answer, and is notified that the request has been held.

Ask one specific question. The text you type is the entire message the requester gets, and it is the text everybody else sees quoted on the request from then on.

What a hold changes

WhatWhile the hold is on
The statusUnchanged underneath — the request is still approved — but every screen shows an On hold badge instead, because the request cannot be paid.
Who it waits onThe requester. The line beside the badge names them.
The payment queueThe request leaves the takeable set and appears on the On hold tab instead.
The reasonQuoted in a banner on the request, and written into the history as a line beginning “On hold:”.

The requester answers in the conversation on the request, in the same comment box everybody else uses. Nothing about their reply lifts the hold — you read it and decide.

Taking a request off hold

On a held request the action bar carries two controls. Keep on hold leaves things as they are — it is there so that reading an answer and deciding it is not enough is a deliberate act rather than an unfinished one. Release hold lifts the hold.

  1. Open the request, either from the On hold tab or from a notification.
  2. Read the reply in History and conversation.
  3. Select Release hold if the answer is good enough.

    No reason is asked for. The hold was the thing that needed explaining; lifting it is answering the question.

  4. The request returns to approved and reappears in the queue for anybody to claim.

    The requester is told the hold is lifted and that nothing further is needed from them.

Hold, or release, or neither

Three tools look similar from a distance. They are not interchangeable.

UseWhenEffect
Put on holdYou need something from the requester before anybody can pay itBlocks payment for everybody until Accounts lifts it
Release reservationYou claimed it but somebody else should pay itHands it back to the open queue — see taking a request for processing
A commentYou want to ask something without blocking anythingAdds to the conversation and blocks nothing — see raising a concern
I cannot find Put on hold on a request.

Either it is not approved, or it is already on hold, or somebody has it claimed. Placing a hold works on an approved, unheld request. If you hold the claim yourself, release it first — the stale-reservation screen says exactly that.

Can the requester take their own request off hold by replying?

No. Only Accounts lifts a hold. Their reply appears in the conversation and the request stays held until somebody in Accounts presses Release hold.

Does a hold stop the approver doing anything?

A hold is about payment: it takes the request out of the payment queue’s takeable set. The approval has already been given by the time you can place one.

The request needs to be cancelled, not held.

Cancelling an approved request is not something Accounts can do. The requester asks for a cancellation and their approver decides it — see asking to cancel an approved request.
